A Guide to the Valley Night Sky

Cosmos • Perspective • Awe

L

We have lost the dark. In our illuminated cities, we see perhaps a few dozen stars. Here in Ogden Valley, designated as an International Dark Sky Park, you can see over 2,500. When you look up, you are not just seeing light; you are seeing time. You are looking back into history, witnessing nuclear fusion that happened millions of years ago.


The Cosmic Perspective

There is a psychological phenomenon known as the "Overview Effect," often reported by astronauts. When seeing Earth from space, their ego dissolves, replaced by a sense of unity and fragility. Stargazing evokes a similar shift. Our terrestrial worries—emails, deadlines, status—seem impossibly small against the backdrop of the Milky Way.


This smallness is not depressing; it is liberating.


What to Look For

From the deck of your yurt, the show changes nightly:

  • Cassiopeia: The Queen, forming a distinct 'W' high in the north.
  • The Pleiades: The Seven Sisters, a tight cluster of blue-hot stars often mistaken for the Little Dipper.
  • Orion: The Hunter, rising in winter to dominate the southern sky.

We provide red-light flashlights (to preserve night vision) and high-powered telescopes. But the best instrument is the naked eye, and patience.
