Embracing Winter Silence

Season • Reflection • Rest

W

inter is often misunderstood as a death. The leaves fall, the water freezes, and the world turns grey. But in nature, winter is not an end; it is a pause. It is the deep inhale before the exhale of spring. In our modern lives, we try to live in eternal summer—constant productivity, constant growth, constant noise.


The Gift of the Dark

When the snow blankets Ogden Valley, a profound hush descends. Sound travels differently here. The sharp edges of the world are softened. This is the season of the kidney meridian in Chinese medicine—a time to store essence, to sleep longer, and to dream deeper.


We invite our guests to lean into the darkness. To light a fire and stare into it for hours. To walk through the snow-laden forest and realize that underneath the cold, the roots are alive and waiting.


Rituals for the Cold

  • Contrast Therapy: From the dry heat of the cedar sauna directly into the snow bank. This shock to the system boosts circulation and dopamine.
  • Candlelight Yoga: Slow, restorative movement that respects the body's need for hibernation.
  • Warm Broths: Bone broths and root vegetable stews that warm from the inside out.
