Foraging in Ogden Valley

Nature • Sustenance • Season

T

There is a profound difference between buying food and finding it. When you purchase a meal, it is a transaction. When you forage, it is a relationship. You are engaging with the land, reading its signs, and accepting its gifts.


Eating Current Sunlight

The philosophy of wild food is often described as "eating current sunlight." Wild plants have not been bred for shelf life or transportability. They are bred for survival. As a result, they are often dense with nutrients and flavor profiles that our modern palates have forgotten.


What We Find

In the high altitudes of Ogden Valley, the seasons dictate the menu.

  • Spring: The erratic thaw brings Morel mushrooms, hidden like treasures in the burn scars and river banks.
  • Summer: Wild berries—Serviceberry, Chokecherry, and Elderberry—ripen in the long days.
  • Autumn: We hunt for Porcini and Pine nuts, preparing the larder for the long quiet.

Our guided foraging walks are not just about identification; they are about ethics. We teach the principle of the "Honorable Harvest"—never taking the first, never taking the last, and always asking permission.
